h1: Supplier sustainability data after the EU Omnibus: what EU customers can still ask Turkish suppliers for
---

Turkish manufacturers that sell to EU customers keep receiving sustainability questionnaires: energy use, emissions, workforce, business conduct, often in a different format from each customer. The EU's Omnibus I Directive changed the rules behind many of these requests in 2026. It narrowed who must report and, for the first time, capped what reporting companies may ask of smaller companies in their value chain.

At the same time, product rules such as CBAM, EUDR, the Packaging Regulation (PPWR), the Battery Regulation and the Cyber Resilience Act ask for their own supplier data, and the cap does not touch them. This guide separates the two kinds of request and shows how a supplier can answer most of them from one base of data.

## What Omnibus I changed

Directive (EU) 2026/470, known as Omnibus I, was published in the Official Journal on 26 February 2026 and entered into force on 18 March 2026. Member States must transpose the reporting changes by 19 March 2027.

- *Fewer reporters.* The Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD) now applies to EU companies and groups with more than 1,000 employees that also exceed a high net turnover threshold. Many companies that had prepared to report no longer have to.
- *Fewer due diligence buyers, later.* The Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD) now covers only very large companies, above 5,000 employees and a much higher turnover threshold, with compliance from 26 July 2029.
- *A cap on supplier requests.* A reporting company may not require a value-chain company with up to 1,000 employees to provide sustainability information beyond what the voluntary standard covers. The supplier may still choose to share more.

## The voluntary standard is now the yardstick

On 3 July 2026 the European Commission adopted revised European Sustainability Reporting Standards and a voluntary reporting standard for companies outside the CSRD. The voluntary standard builds on EFRAG's VSME and was published as Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/1560 on 21 September 2026. According to the published text, the cap applies to financial years starting on or after 1 January 2027.

The standard has a basic module and a comprehensive module. The basic module covers general information, energy and greenhouse gas emissions, pollution, biodiversity, water, resource use and waste, the workforce, health and safety, pay and training, and corruption. A mid-sized Turkish manufacturer can usually prepare it from data it already holds in finance, operations and HR.

## Two kinds of request, two different rules

The cap applies only to information a customer needs for its own sustainability report. It does not limit what a customer must collect to meet other EU laws, such as product, import or due diligence rules. Those requests follow their own regulations, and in most cases the customer cannot place the product on the EU market without the answer.

| Request | Comes from | Is it under the cap? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Energy, emissions, workforce, business conduct questionnaire | Your customer's CSRD report | Yes, if you have up to 1,000 employees |
| Actual embedded emissions per installation, with a verifier's report | CBAM (steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen, electricity) | No |
| Geolocation of plots and evidence for the due diligence statement | EUDR (cattle, cocoa, coffee, oil palm, rubber, soya, wood) | No |
| Technical documentation, declaration of conformity, substance limits | PPWR (packaging) | No |
| Data for the battery passport and raw-material due diligence | Battery Regulation | No |
| Vulnerability handling and security update information | Cyber Resilience Act | No |
| Evidence that products are not made with forced labour | Forced Labour Regulation and buyer due diligence | No |

For the product rules, see our rule pages on [CBAM](/en/insights/cbam), [EUDR](/en/insights/eudr), [PPWR](/en/insights/ppwr) and the [Cyber Resilience Act](/en/insights/cra), and the [questions EU buyers ask](/en/insights/eu-buyer-questions).

## What a supplier should prepare

1. *List last year's requests.* Note who asked, in what format, and which rule each request comes from: a customer's sustainability report or a product rule.
2. *Check which customers still report.* Customers that fell out of the CSRD may still ask, but on a voluntary basis. Customers that remain in scope must respect the cap from financial year 2027.
3. *Prepare one base report along the voluntary standard.* Use the basic module as the core and add comprehensive-module items only where your largest customers need them.
4. *Use the same numbers everywhere.* Energy and emissions figures in the base report, in CBAM data and in a TSRS report should come from the same source and match.
5. *Name an owner for each figure.* Finance, operations, HR and sales usually each hold part of the data; one person should own the file.

Larger Turkish companies may also report under the Turkish Sustainability Reporting Standards (TSRS) of the Public Oversight, Accounting and Auditing Standards Authority (KGK), which updated its thresholds in January 2026. A TSRS report covers much of the same ground, but customers often ask in their own format and at product or site level, so a base file mapped to both saves repeated work.

:::faq
### Can we refuse a customer's sustainability questionnaire?
If you have up to 1,000 employees and the request goes beyond the voluntary standard, you may decline the extra part when the cap applies, from financial year 2027. Commercially, a short answer from a ready base report usually works better than a refusal.
### Does the cap cover CBAM or EUDR data?
No. The cap concerns information for sustainability reporting only. CBAM emissions data, EUDR geolocation and due diligence evidence, and other product-rule data are required by their own regulations.
### Do we have to publish a report under the voluntary standard?
No. The standard is voluntary. Preparing it gives you one file to answer most customer requests, and you decide whom to share it with.
### Does a TSRS report answer EU customers?
Often in part. It uses a different framework, and customers may ask for product- or site-level figures. Keep both built on the same underlying data.
:::
