On 13 November 2026 the European Commission posted a "State-of-play CBAM accreditation" table on its Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) verification page. It shows which national accreditation bodies (NABs) accredit CBAM verifiers, but does not name them or give a headcount.

For a Turkish steel, aluminium, cement or fertiliser plant that gap matters. In 2026 your EU buyer's authorised CBAM declarant will declare embedded emissions on default values or on your installation's actual, verified emissions (see our [default-versus-actual choice](/en/insights/cbam-default-values-2026-turkish-steel-aluminium)). Actual values need a verifier's report for 2026. The first annual declaration and certificate surrender are due by 30 September 2027 (2023/956, as amended by 2025/2083, Articles 6 and 22; Commission verification page).

Ganrich is not a verification body.

## Verification is what lets a buyer use actual values instead of defaults

From 1 January 2026, embedded emissions may be determined from actual emissions or from default values (Regulation (EU) 2023/956, Article 7, as amended). Verification is required only where actual emissions are used (Article 8(1)) and is done at the installation that produces the goods (Commission verification page). The operator calculates embedded emissions, has them verified, keeps a copy of the report for four years, and may disclose it through the CBAM Registry to an authorised CBAM declarant; the Commission and national competent authorities may review it (Articles 8 and 10(5)–(7)). The legal obligation sits with the EU importer, not the Turkish producer, but the data and the report come from the plant. For certificate prices, see our [CBAM 2026 update](/en/insights/cbam-2026-update-tr-ets-cop31) and the [Türkiye ETS market and registry guide](/en/insights/turkiye-ets-market-registry-cbam).

## Who is allowed to verify?

Only a NAB of an EU Member State can grant CBAM accreditation. EEA NABs may do so only after the CBAM Regulation is incorporated into the EEA Agreement (Commission verification page). TÜRKAK cannot grant EU CBAM accreditation on its own.

Under Article 3 of Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551, a legal person established in a Member State applies, as a rule, to that state's NAB, and may go elsewhere only if that NAB does not offer the service. A person established outside the EU may apply to any Member State NAB that offers the service, by Annex I activity group (I–VIII for goods; L–LII for CCUS, electricity, indirect emissions).

A verification body established in Türkiye can verify CBAM data only if an EU NAB has accredited it for the relevant group. ISO 14064, ISO 14065 or EU ETS accreditation under 2018/2067 is not enough: a relevant ETS accreditation is taken into account, but recognition is not automatic (2023/956, Article 18(2), as amended; Commission guidance, 24 August 2026, section 9.2.4). Once accredited, the verifier must apply for Registry registration with the competent authority of the NAB's Member State within two months, and not before 1 September 2026 (Article 10a). An accreditation certificate is valid for no more than five years (2025/2551, Article 6(3)).

## What the 13 November 2026 table shows

The 13 November 2026 PDF totals, from EA information, are: **24** NABs have agreed to provide CBAM accreditation; **20** are ready to accept applications; **7** have agreed to accredit third-country applicants; **7** are already accepting those applications.

The seven already accepting third-country applications are France (COFRAC), Greece (ESYD), Italy (Accredia), the Netherlands (RvA), Poland (PCA), Slovakia (SNAS) and Sweden (Swedac). The Czech Republic (CAI), Hungary (NAH) and Romania (RENAR) have agreed but are not yet accepting applications. Five NABs (Cyprus, Estonia, Iceland, Ireland, Malta) declined.

In the Dutch row, the third-country column reads "Yes + Agreement with TK’s TURKAK". The PDF does not describe what the agreement legally does. On 25 June 2026 RvA and TÜRKAK announced on LinkedIn a cooperation agreement on CBAM accreditation, signed by RvA chair Roeland Nieuweboer and TÜRKAK Secretary General Banu Müderrisoğlu; we could not find the text on turkak.org.tr or rva.nl as of 16 November 2026. The note does not mean TÜRKAK can grant EU CBAM accreditation.

The Commission verification page still says the list of accredited CBAM verifiers "will be published on this page". As of 18 November 2026 there is no named list there. The only official NAB announcement we have read is RvA's 10 November 2026 notice that it has accredited Integrated Sustainability Solutions Company (ISSC); the notice does not state the scope, and the EU-wide headcount is unknown. Before signing, the minimum checks remain the certificate, scope and Registry registration proof.

## The calendar: monitor in 2026, report and declare in 2027

- **January 2026:** importers apply for authorisation; operators start monitoring and calculating embedded emissions.
- **April 2026:** NABs begin CBAM accreditation.
- **September 2026:** accredited verifiers may register in the CBAM Registry; first verifications (documentation review and a site visit) can start (Article 10a).
- **January 2027:** verifiers may issue the first reports in the Registry; operators may share them with declarants (Commission verification page).
- **1 February 2027:** declarants may buy CBAM certificates (Article 20(1), as amended).
- **30 September 2027:** first annual CBAM declaration and surrender for the year 2026 (Articles 6 and 22).

The O3CI portal is already open (Registry page guides, February 2026). A declarant can retrieve installation data only if it has shared its EORI number with the supplier outside the Registry (CBAM Registry page, 16 November 2026).

## Five questions to ask a verifier

1. **What is your activity-group scope, and may I see the certificate?** Accreditation follows Annex I to 2025/2551 (steel products typically group V or VIII; aluminium VII or VIII).
2. **Are you registered in the CBAM Registry, and which competent authority approved the registration?** Accreditation is not enough without the Article 10a registration; reports are issued in the Registry from January 2027.
3. **How will you plan the physical site visit for 2026?** Ask for a written plan on dates, team and language (see below).
4. **In which language will the visit and the working file run?** The operator's carbon price report must be in English (2026/2498, Article 8(3)). Ask whether the team can work in Turkish on site.
5. **Have you provided consultancy on our monitoring plan or emissions calculation?** Consulting by the verifier, or any part of the same legal entity, on the monitoring plan, methodology or emissions report is an unacceptable impartiality risk (2025/2551, Annex II, section 1.7). After five consecutive years the lead auditor must step aside for at least three years (Annex II, section 1.7.6).

## What the verifier will actually check

Verification is performed to a reasonable-assurance standard: whether the operator's report is free from material misstatements and material non-conformities (Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/2551, Annex II). Materiality is **5%** of total specific embedded emissions, and 5% of total specific embedded free allocation, for each good identified by CN code (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2546, Article 5). That is not a tolerance band; misstatements should be corrected before the report is issued where possible (Commission guidance, 24 August 2026, section 3.5).

Recital 3 of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2546 is explicit: **in the first year subject to verification, a physical site visit should be required in all cases.** A virtual visit or waiver under Article 3 depends on a prior physical visit; for most installations 2026 is year one, so Article 3 is closed as a rule.

## New: 2026/2498 and certifying the carbon price paid (16 November 2026)

On 16 November 2026 the Official Journal published Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2498. It enters into force on 19 November 2026 and applies from 1 January 2026 (Article 36). It sets how a carbon price effectively paid in a third country is converted into a reduction in the CBAM certificates to be surrendered. Annex II, which is not exhaustive, lists the Türkiye Emission Trading System as an emissions trading system, with free allowances as the main type of rebate. Being listed confirms only that it counts as a carbon price mechanism (Article 4(2)); it does not by itself create a deduction.

For the 2026 and 2027 reporting periods, a CBAM verifier accredited for the relevant activity group may opt to act as the 'independent person' that certifies the operator's carbon price report, without separate accreditation. It requests the role from the competent authority through the CBAM Registry and, once validated, notifies its accreditation body (Article 11(1)). The role ceases by 30 September 2028 unless the verifier is accredited for the carbon-price scope in Annex IV, and it can be revoked earlier (Article 11(3) and (5)). After that, certification requires Annex IV accreditation (Article 10(1)); the Commission describes it as existing verifiers extending their scope (Commission news, 16 November 2026).

A deduction can rest on a carbon price effectively paid, certified by an independent person, which requires embedded emissions based on actual values (recital 5), or on a default carbon price the Commission will publish in the CBAM Registry (Article 7(2); Commission news, 16 November 2026). Emissions covered by free allowances do not count as price paid (Article 9(1)(b)). For TR ETS, 2026 is reporting-only with 100% free allocation, and allowance surrender starts with 2027 emissions (Carbon Market Board decision KPK/2026/1; Climate Change Presidency, 1 October 2026). The only Turkish payment in prospect for 2026 emissions is a planned voluntary supplementary allowance payment from 2027, whose method is not yet published (ICAP, 8 September 2026). Our assessment: a meaningful deduction for 2026 is unlikely unless that payment is made and recognised as a price paid.

## Planning 2026 verification now

1. Confirm the CN codes and production routes in scope ([CBAM readiness check](/en/decision-lab/cbam-readiness-check)).
2. Finish 2026 monitoring and a monitoring plan under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2547.
3. If needed, register the operator and installation through the O3CI portal.
4. Identify the matching CBAM activity group (I–VIII, and L–LII if relevant).
5. Shortlist verifiers accredited, or completing accreditation, for that group. For a firm established in Türkiye, ask for a certificate from one of the seven NABs accepting third-country applications (13 November), and use the Commission list once published. Put the five questions above in writing and file the certificate, scope, expiry and Registry registration evidence, including whether the firm will certify the carbon price paid.
6. Contract early: the first year includes a physical site visit, and the report can be issued in the Registry only from January 2027. Host the visit and correct flagged misstatements before the report is issued.
7. Prepare the monitoring plan, change logs, emissions report, sampling plan, prior-period data, databases and precursor evidence.
8. From January 2027, have the report issued in the Registry and open it to EU declarants by sharing their EORI numbers.

## FAQ

:::faq
### Can a Turkish certification body verify CBAM data?
Only if an EU NAB has accredited it for the relevant activity group. TÜRKAK accreditation alone is not enough (see above).
### Is there an official list of accredited verifiers?
Not on the Commission page as of 18 November 2026 (see above).
### Does EU ETS or ISO 14064 verification count?
Not by itself. Relevant ETS accreditation is taken into account, but it is not a substitute for CBAM accreditation (see above).
### Is a site visit mandatory in 2026?
Yes. In the first year a physical site visit is required as a rule (see above).
### Who pays, and who owns the report?
The fee is contractual. The operator must ensure verification, keep a copy for four years and may disclose it to a declarant (Article 10(5)–(7)).
### Can my verifier also certify the carbon price paid?
For the 2026 and 2027 reporting periods, yes, if it opts in: an accredited CBAM verifier can request the independent-person role through the CBAM Registry without separate accreditation. The role ceases by 30 September 2028 unless it is accredited for the carbon-price scope in Annex IV (2026/2498, Articles 10–11). For Turkish installations a meaningful 2026 deduction is unlikely; see above.
### Does the RvA–TÜRKAK agreement mean TÜRKAK can accredit CBAM verifiers?
No. Only EU NABs — and EEA NABs once the Regulation is incorporated into the EEA Agreement — can grant CBAM accreditation (see above).
:::
