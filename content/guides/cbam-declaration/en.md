h1: CBAM declaration 2027: what the Commission's draft rules mean for Turkish suppliers
---

On 19 November 2026 the European Commission published a draft implementing regulation "on CBAM declarations" (reference Ares(2026)10806938) on its Have Your Say portal. It sets out how the annual CBAM declaration is filed and revised, and how the certificates owed are calculated, rounded and surrendered. Feedback is open until 17 December 2026.

Its cover says it "has not been adopted or endorsed by the European Commission", so it can change. It is still the clearest signal yet of what an EU buyer will need from a Turkish steel, aluminium, cement or fertiliser supplier before the first declaration falls due on 30 September 2027.

This piece is for the supplier side: you will not file the declaration, but much of what goes into it has to come from you.

## What was published, and on what legal basis

The draft is based on Articles 6(6) and 14(6) of the CBAM Regulation, (EU) 2023/956. Under draft Article 1 it covers the standard format of the declaration, the procedure for submitting it through the CBAM Registry, and the arrangements for surrendering certificates, including how the declarant picks which ones. It has 16 articles and an Annex listing the declaration's data elements, down to each emissions line. Until the act is adopted, the rules in force are those in Regulation 2023/956 as amended by the 2025 simplification Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2025/2083.

## The first declaration: 2026 imports, due 30 September 2027

Under Article 6(1) of the amended Regulation, every authorised CBAM declarant files a declaration in the CBAM Registry by 30 September each year for the previous calendar year, for the first time in 2027 for the year 2026. Article 22(1) sets the same date for surrender; draft recital 2 confirms the 2027 start.

The declarant is an EU party. It is either the importer or, where the importer is not established in the EU, an indirect customs representative (Arts 5(1) and 5(2) of 2023/956). Draft Article 2(2) requires the declarant to state in which of three capacities it files: as importer, as indirect customs representative acting for an importer, or as a person acting in the declarant's name under Article 5(7a).

A Turkish exporter therefore does not file, but the declaration is built from data only the producing installation holds.

## Actual or default, element by element

Draft Article 6(2) requires the declarant to state, for each relevant element, whether it uses **actual values or default values** to determine three things:

1. the embedded emissions of the goods;
2. the adjustment for free allocation in the EU ETS;
3. where relevant, the reduction for a carbon price paid in a third country.

Where default values are used, draft Article 6(3) has the Registry fill in the figures automatically once the declarant enters the CN code and country of origin. The figures are the default values in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2621, the free allocation benchmarks in Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2620 and the default carbon prices for the year. Defaults save data collection but usually cost more certificates; our [CBAM default values article](/en/insights/cbam-default-values-2026-turkish-steel-aluminium) explains why.

Where actual values are used, the draft is specific about the evidence (draft Article 8):

- **If your installation is registered in the CBAM Registry** (Article 10 of 2023/956) and you have disclosed your verified data to the buyer under Article 10(7), the declarant only has to *associate* your verification report with its declaration in the Registry (draft Art. 8(2)).
- **If your installation is not registered**, the declarant must *upload* both the verification report and the operator's emissions report that goes with it (draft Art. 8(3)). Draft Article 7(2) refers to an authenticated verification report issued in the Registry and an emissions report "based on the electronic template provided in the CBAM registry".

Per emissions line, the Annex asks for the production country, production year, emissions type (default or actual), a verification report link, and the specific direct and indirect embedded emissions per unit. Draft Article 7(1) adds supporting evidence for the installation's reporting period under Article 7(1) of Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/2547.

In practice, Registry registration and a verifier report accepted in the Registry decide how easily your buyer can use your actual figures. Our [verifier selection guide](/en/insights/cbam-verifier-turkish-installations-2026) covers that step.

## Revision, rounding and surrender: the rules that set the final bill

Four draft rules decide what the buyer finally pays.

**Revision until 30 September.** Under draft Article 2(4), a declaration may be revised and resubmitted until 30 September of the filing year, provided the certificates for it have not yet been surrendered. The latest version replaces the earlier one. So if your verified figures arrive late, the buyer can still correct the declaration, but only until it surrenders.

**Non-binding compliance checks.** Under draft Article 3, the competent authority may flag "apparent errors, inconsistencies, omissions" before surrender. Such recommendations are non-binding and do not amount to approval. The declarant stays responsible for accuracy.

**One formula, one rounding.** Draft Article 12(2) sets the calculation for goods other than electricity: quantity × specific embedded emissions, minus the specific embedded free allocation and, where relevant, the carbon price paid. Draft Article 13 forbids rounding of intermediate values. Only the final total is rounded to a whole certificate: below 0.5 down, 0.5 and above up (Art. 13(4); recital 5).

**Free choice of certificates, under watch.** Draft Article 14(1) lets the declarant choose which certificates in its account to surrender. Under Art. 14(2), an indirect customs representative may surrender separately per importer. Recital 6 warns that the Commission will monitor this freedom and may restrict it, for example by requiring surrender "in chronological order of purchase", if it sees circumvention.

Missing certificates must be surrendered "without delay", without prejudice to Article 26 penalties (draft Art. 15). If no declaration is filed, the authority can demand one and, failing that, set the number itself (draft Art. 4).

For supplier contracts, Article 26(1a) of the amended Regulation allows the authority to reduce a penalty where the declarant got the number wrong "as a result of incorrect information provided by a third party, namely an operator, a verifier" or the person certifying a carbon price. EU buyers are likely to ask for data warranties.

## Carbon price paid: where Türkiye stands for 2026

Draft Article 6 sets out how a carbon price paid abroad enters the calculation. Where default values are used for embedded emissions, the Registry also makes the year's default carbon prices available, alongside the default values and benchmarks (draft Art. 6(3)(c)). Where actual values are used, draft Article 6(4) gives the declarant two options: the Commission's default carbon price, applied automatically in the Registry, or the price actually paid, backed by a certification report from an independent person under Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2498 (draft Arts 6(4)(b) and 8(4)). Draft Article 12(3)(c) applies the default carbon prices "where applicable". The route based on the price effectively paid rests on Article 9(1) of 2023/956, which applies "where the embedded emissions are determined on the basis of actual emissions".

For 2026 goods from Türkiye, this deduction looks unlikely to matter much. The Turkish ETS pilot covers 2026 with reporting obligations only. The first surrender obligation applies to 2027 emissions (İklim Değişikliği Başkanlığı, 1 October 2026). Under Carbon Market Board decision KPK/2026/1, a method for a complementary allowance price is being developed in 2026, and the price mechanism starts from the 2027 system year (İklim Değişikliği Başkanlığı, 3 September 2026). ICAP describes this as a voluntary supplementary allowance payment planned from 2027 (8 September 2026). Whether such a payment would count as a carbon price paid is not settled. Our [TR-ETS article](/en/insights/turkiye-ets-market-registry-cbam) explains the timeline.

## What a Turkish supplier should prepare: a data pack checklist

The draft's Annex is a good template for what a buyer will ask for. Items an EU customer is likely to need before it starts its 2026 file:

1. **Trade data, matched.** For each shipment: CN code (8 digits), net mass in tonnes, country of origin, and the invoice and customs references the buyer will reconcile against its import declarations.
2. **Installation identity.** Installation name, address and production country, plus the production year for each batch shipped in 2026.
3. **Actual or default, by product.** Whether verified actual data will be available per CN code, and when; if not, flagged early. The Registry fills in defaults.
4. **Specific embedded emissions.** Direct and indirect, in tCO2e per tonne, calculated under Implementing Regulation 2025/2547, with the reporting period used and evidence for it (draft Art. 7(1)).
5. **Verification.** A report from an accredited verifier, plus the operator's emissions report on the Registry template. Registered installations disclose the data to the buyer's account (Art. 10(7) of 2023/956); unregistered ones hand over both documents for upload (draft Art. 8(3)).
6. **Timing.** Verified figures with the buyer well before 30 September 2027. The buyer can revise only until it surrenders (draft Art. 2(4)).
7. **Records.** Verification report and calculation records kept for four years after the verification (Art. 10(5)(c) of 2023/956). Registry data is kept for seven years (draft Art. 16).
8. **Contract terms.** Written agreement on who delivers what, by when, and what happens if a figure is later corrected.

## Commenting before 17 December

Have Your Say feedback on the draft is open to the public until 17 December 2026, midnight Brussels time; contributors need to register or log in (initiative page, accessed 25 November 2026). Two points a supplier, or its sector association, might raise: how the verification report of an unregistered installation is to be authenticated in the Registry, and whether the template for the operator's emissions report will be published well before 2027.

## Open points to watch

- **Adoption and timing.** The draft is undated ("of XXX") and the committee opinion is blank (recital 10). The initiative page lists Commission adoption as planned for the first quarter of 2027. The rules will not apply until the act is adopted and published.
- **Entry into force.** Recital 9 says the day after publication. The final article says the twentieth day.
- **Unfinished cross-references.** Article 7 still contains placeholders on the carbon price certification report and the electricity addendum.
- **Default carbon prices.** The Registry pre-fill under draft Article 6(3)(c) depends on a Commission implementing decision for the year concerned. We found none published as of 25 November 2026.
- **The 2028 downstream extension.** If CBAM reaches finished goods from 2028, the same declaration logic will reach machinery and parts makers. More in our [2028 downstream article](/en/insights/cbam-downstream-extension-2028-turkish-manufacturers).

## FAQ

:::faq
### Does a Turkish exporter file the CBAM declaration?
No. The authorised CBAM declarant in the EU files it: the importer, or an indirect customs representative if the importer is not established in the EU (Art. 5 of 2023/956; draft Art. 2(2)). The supplier provides the emissions data and evidence.
### When is the first declaration due?
By 30 September 2027, for goods imported in 2026 (Arts 6(1) and 22(1) of 2023/956 as amended by 2025/2083; draft recital 2).
### Is the draft binding now?
No. It is a consultation draft that has not been adopted. Until adoption, the rules in Regulation 2023/956 as amended apply.
### What if my buyer uses default values?
The Registry fills in the default value, the free allocation benchmark and the default carbon price for the CN code and origin (draft Art. 6(3)). That usually means more certificates than verified actual data would require.
### Who can comment on the draft, and until when?
Have Your Say feedback is open to the public until 17 December 2026, midnight Brussels time. Contributors need to register or log in.
:::
